# Timing and word budgets (quick reference)

## Words per length

| Length | 110 wpm (AZ/TR/RU, calm) | 130 wpm | 150 wpm (EN, lively) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 15 s | 25–28 | 30–33 | 35–38 |
| 30 s | 55 | 65 | 75 |
| 45 s | 80 | 95–100 | 110 |
| 60 s | 110 | 130 | 150 |
| 90 s | 165 | 195 | 225 |
| 3 min | 330 | 390 | 450 |
| 5 min | 550 | 650 | 750 |
| 10 min | 1100 | 1300 | 1500 |
| 20 min | 2200 | 2600 | 3000 |
| 30 min | 3300 | 3900 | 4500 |

- Subtract about 2–3 words for each long pause `//` and 1 word for each `/` in short videos.
- Music-only intro or outro time comes off the budget.
- Talking-point podcasts (not word-for-word) run about 20–30% longer than the word count suggests; plan fewer points.

## Formula

`words = seconds ÷ 60 × wpm`
`seconds = words ÷ wpm × 60`

Example: a 210-word script at 140 wpm → 210 ÷ 140 × 60 = 90 s.

## Segment plan for common episode lengths

| Episode | Cold open | Intro | Segments | Recap + outro |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 min solo | 0:15 | 0:30 | 3 × 2:45 | 0:45 |
| 20 min solo | 0:20 | 0:40 | 4 × 4:30 | 1:00 |
| 30 min interview | 0:30 | 1:00 | warm-up 3:00, core 22:00, closing 2:30 | 1:00 |

## Hook bank (patterns; write new words each time)

- A surprising fact `[check source]`.
- The listener's problem in their words: "You studied English for years and still freeze when speaking?"
- A promise with a number: "In 60 seconds, 3 ways to…"
- A tiny story: "Last year I failed my first interview. Here is what changed."
- A question they want answered.

## Reading check

- Read the script aloud with a timer once. If it runs over by more than 10%, cut, don't speed up.
- Mark breaths every 1–2 sentences.
- End sentences on the important word; that is where the voice naturally stresses.
